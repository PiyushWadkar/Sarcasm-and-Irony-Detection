Project Abstract & Problem Statement

Figurative language—specifically sarcasm and irony—poses one of the most formidable challenges in Natural Language Processing (NLP). Standard sentiment analysis models operate on literal semantic assumptions: positive sentiment words (e.g., "great", "love", "wonderful") are assigned positive polarities. However, in sarcastic discourse, speakers deliberately invert polarity to express derision, contempt, or frustration (e.g., "I just love waiting in 4-hour airport queues").

This project implements an end-to-end NLP and machine learning system that differentiates between Sarcastic (verbal irony realized through polarity contrast), Ironical (situational or rhetorical incongruity without direct polarity flip), and Non-Sarcastic / Literal text. It integrates transparent probability calibration and linear model feature attribution to explain why predictions are made.

Sarcasm vs Irony: Linguistic Taxonomy
1. Sarcasm (Verbal Irony with Polarity Contrast)
A direct clash between surface semantics and speaker intention, often intended to mock or ridicule. "Brilliant job crashing the server on launch day!"
2. Irony (Situational & Figurative Incongruity)
A state of affairs or an event that seems deliberately contrary to what one expects. "A fire station burning down" or "A pilot having a fear of heights".
3. Literal / Non-Sarcastic
Statements where the intended communicative meaning directly mirrors literal dictionary definitions. "The library closes at 8 PM tonight."

B.Tech Viva Voce & Technical Defense Guide

Standard stopword lists remove negation words like not, never, no, hardly, and intensifiers like so, very, too. In sarcasm and sentiment analysis, negations are the core semantic pivots that invert polarity (e.g. "not great" vs "great"). Stripping them destroys polarity contrast. Our custom preprocessor implements an irony-aware stopword filter that explicitly preserves contrastive conjunctions, negations, and intensifiers.

Sarcasm is considered a sub-type of verbal irony specifically characterized by a polarity clash between a positive lexical surface and a negative pragmatic intention, often directed toward a person or event to ridicule. General irony encompasses situational incongruity (events contrary to expectation) and dramatic irony without an explicit polarity inversion. SemEval-2018 Task 3 formalized this distinction in Subtask B, which this system implements.

A standard Linear SVM optimizes the geometric margin between hyperplanes and outputs an uncalibrated signed decision distance \(f(x) \in (-\infty, \infty)\), not a true posterior probability \(P(y=c|x)\). Platt scaling (implemented via CalibratedClassifierCV) fits a logistic sigmoid over cross-validation decision margins, yielding statistically sound probabilities between 0% and 100% without fabricating values.

For linear classifiers, the classification decision is determined by the linear combination of feature weights and TF-IDF terms: \(\text{score}_c = \sum_i w_{c,i} \cdot x_i\). For any input sentence, the system extracts the active n-grams present in the sentence, multiplies each n-gram's TF-IDF value by the learned coefficient for the predicted class, and highlights the highest positive contributing tokens.

Current n-gram bag-of-words models lack deep conversational context, speaker background, and multimodal cues (acoustic pitch, facial expressions, tone of voice). Future scope involves fine-tuning transformer architectures (e.g. RoBERTa or DeBERTa) with conversational context windows and extending into multimodal sarcasm detection with video/audio speech signals.
